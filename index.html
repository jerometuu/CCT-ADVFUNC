Parttime break - Quadratic Function

Transformations

I chose this photo because during a break time at my part-time job, I noticed the curved handle of my co-worker's water bottle. To model the handle appearing in the photo, I began by identifying that its shape closely resembled a power function with an even-like behavior, so I selected the parent function $y = x^{2.4}$ as a starting point. From observing the orientation of the curve in my image, I noticed that it was opening downwards rather than upwards, which indicated a reflection across the x-axis. I therefore modified the function to $y = -x^{2.4}$ to match the direction of the graph.

Initially, it was challenging to balance the vertical stretch and horizontal compression because the curve needed to perfectly span between both sides of the bottle cap while reaching the correct peak. By observing the width of the handle, I applied a horizontal stretch by a factor of 2, modifying the input to $\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2.4}$. To scale the curve to the exact height of the handle, I applied a vertical stretch by a factor of 2.6. Finally, to position the peak of the handle at its actual height above the bottle cap, I applied a vertical translation of 1.3 units upward. This resulted in the function

$$y = 2.6 \cdot \left(0.5 - \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2.4}\right)$$

which closely models the shape in the photograph.

Summary of Transformations:

  • Reflection on the x-axis: $y = x^{2.4} \rightarrow y = -x^{2.4}$
  • Horizontal stretch by a factor of 2: $y = -\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2.4}$
  • Vertical stretch by a factor of 2.6: $y = -2.6\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2.4}$
  • Vertical translation of 1.3 units upward: $y = -2.6\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2.4} + 1.3$

Domain and Range

The domain of my function is restricted to $D: \{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid -1.18 \le x \le 1.18\}$. I limited the domain to the portion of the handle that was physically visible and connected to the bottle cap in the photograph. As a result, my model is only valid for the curve of the handle captured in the image.

The parent function $y = x^{2.4}$ has a natural domain of all real numbers $D: \{x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ and a range of $R: \{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid y \ge 0\}$. Unlike rational or logarithmic functions, this parent function does not possess any asymptotes, meaning the function is continuous everywhere.

Since the function is continuous on the restricted interval and perfectly symmetrical around the y-axis ($x = 0$), the maximum value occurs at the vertex where $x = 0$, yielding a height of $1.3\text{ cm}$. The minimum values occur at the endpoints of the domain. By evaluating the function at the boundaries, $x = -1.18$ and $x = 1.18$, I obtained the minimum approximate output value of $0.5\text{ cm}$, which aligns right at the top level of the bottle cap.

Therefore, the range of the function for this specific situation is approximately $R: \{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid 0.5 \le y \le 1.3\}$.

Post-Exam Rush - Absolute Value Function

Transformations

I chose this photo because right after finishing a challenging Data Management math test, I found myself in a state of absolute urgency, holding back an incredibly intense stomach ache. Running out of the classroom in desperate search of the nearest restroom, this directional campus arrow caught my eye. To mathematically capture the geometric, sharp point of the indicator sign, I determined that its structural pattern closely mirrored an absolute value function, electing $y = |x|$ as my parent function.

Looking at the layout on the coordinate system, the peak of the arrow points upwards but slopes symmetrically downwards into an inverted 'V' shape, signifying a reflection across the x-axis. To match this direction, I updated the model to $y = -|x|$. Through careful calibration against the slope of the visual marker, I discovered that the angle was slightly wider than the parent path, requiring a vertical compression by a factor of 0.8 to give me $y = -0.8|x|$. Lastly, to vertically position the absolute peak of the directional sign to its exact coordinate value above the baseline, I applied a vertical translation of 5 units upward, arriving at the finalized function

$$y = -0.8|x| + 5$$

which maps perfectly onto the architectural signage captured during that stressful moment.

Summary of Transformations:

  • Reflection on the x-axis: $y = |x| \rightarrow y = -|x|$
  • Vertical compression by a factor of 0.8: $y = -0.8|x|$
  • Vertical translation of 5 units upward: $y = -0.8|x| + 5$

Domain and Range

The domain for this particular function is restricted to the closed boundaries of $D: \{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid -3 \le x \le 3\}$. This specific mathematical interval limits the graph to only show the structural arrowhead shape itself as defined within my Desmos parameters, preventing the lines from extending infinitely down across the rest of the blue background panel.

The standard unconstrained parent function $y = |x|$ possesses a domain encompassing all real numbers $D: \{x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ and a non-negative range defined by $R: \{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid y \ge 0\}$. It maintains a continuous domain across its entire structure with no fractional gaps, asymptotic behavior, or undefined regions.

Because this model operates along a continuous bounded domain and is symmetric about the axis of reflection, the absolute maximum point of the function lands squarely on the vertex coordinate at $(0,5)$, marking a peak height of $5\text{ cm}$. The lowest geometric extensions are located at the domain's outer bounds. Evaluating the boundary coordinates where $x = -3$ and $x = 3$ gives an identical minimum output height computation of $-0.8|3| + 5 = 2.6\text{ cm}$.
